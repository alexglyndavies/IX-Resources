    AI in Science Learning Resources¶

    A curated collection of books, courses, tutorials, and community resources for scientists and engineers using artificial intelligence and machine learning in their research.

    This is intended as a starting point for learning and exploration rather than an exhaustive catalogue. Some resources span several topics, and the collection will continue to evolve.

    Resources developed from the Schmidt AI in Science Fellowship¶

    The following resources emerged from various workshops and conferences led and organized by the Schmidt AI in Science Fellows.

    • Foundation Model for Science Workshop (2025, U Toronto): Tutorials are in the context of protein language models.

    • Symbolic Model Discovery from Time-Series Data (2025, Imperial College London): Lecture recordings and tutorials on PySR and PySINDy.

    • Knowledge-Guided Machine Learning (2025, U Michigan)

    • Pre-workshop materials (sample data and notebooks)

    • Keynote talks

    • Tutorials

    • Ten simple rules for navigating AI in science (2025)

    • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Copilot for Scientific Software (2025): Tutorial on building an LLM-powered, domain-specific Q&A chatbot using RAG. This tutorial was offered for the Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellows by the eScience Institute at the University of Washington. A video recording is also available.

    • EcoViz (2024, UC San Diego): “A geospatial hub for multidimensional visualizations and data storytelling about impacts to ecosystems and nature-based solutions. From animal behavior to natural flood protection, we showcase biological resilience as a source for hope amidst global change.”

    General Learning Resources¶

    Machine Learning¶

    Books¶

    • Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning by Christopher M. Bishop. This book is a great introduction to various machine learning methods, it is aimed at advanced undergraduates or first-year PhD students, as well as researchers and practitioners, and assumes no previous knowledge of pattern recognition or machine learning.

    • Deep Learning by I. Goodfellow, Y. Bengio and A. Courville. Authored by pioneers in the field, freely available. Probably the best book on Deep Learning.

    • Dive into Deep Learning by Aston Zhang et al. This book includes coding tutorials and samples.

    • Deep Learning, Foundations and Concepts by Bishop and Bishop. Provides an exhaustive overview of the topic. Accessible to beginners but includes material for advanced learners.

    Online Courses¶

    • Supervised Machine Learning: Regression and Classification by Andrew Ng. This is a great introduction course on supervised machine learning. The various models/algorithms are first introduced and then applied using Python.

    • Unsupervised Learning, Recommenders, Reinforcement Learning by Andrew Ng. This is a great follow-up to the course above. This course introduces (and applies using Python) various unsupervised machine learning models/algorithms.

    • Deep Learning Specialization by Andrew Ng This is a 5-part specialisation course that introduces (and applies using Python) various deep learning models, from a simple Neural Network to more advanced Recurrent Neural Networks.

    • Practical Deep Learning for Coders from fastai. Associated textbook (“Deep Learning for Coders with fastai & PyTorch: AI Applications without a PhD”).

    • The University of Tübingen ML YouTube channel.

    Tutorials¶

    • Insightful introductions to topics in Deep Learning can also be found at https://distill.pub/.

    • The notebooks by UvA DL are a great introduction to a broad set of topics within ML.

    Applied Statistics¶

    Books¶

    • Statistical Rethinking: A Bayesian Course with Examples in R and Stan by Richard McElreath. This is a great introduction to Bayesian statistics for both PhD students and seasoned professionals in the natural and social sciences, and it prepares them for more advanced or specialised statistical modeling. The author provides additional resources, which include online lectures, R code, and Python code.

    • Bayesian Data Analysis by Andrew Gelman, John B. Carlin, Hal S. Stern, and Donald B. Rubin. This book covers a wide range of topics in Bayesian statistics, from single-parameter estimation to non-parametric priors. Additional supporting resources include lectures and code in R, Python, and MATLAB.

    • The Elements of Statistical Learning by Jerome H. Friedman, Robert Tibshirani, and Trevor Hastie. This book covers a wide range of topics in both statistics and machine learning. The book is aimed at people who are interested in “learning from data”.

    • A Student’s Guide to Bayesian Statistics by B Lambert

    Online Courses¶

    • Statistics and Probability by Khan Academy An excellent introduction to Statistics and Probability aimed at high school students. The course covers a wide range of useful topics, such as hypothesis testing.

    • Statistical Rethinking by Richard McElreath. The course gives a comprehensive and easily accessible introduction to Bayesian statistics. See also the list of books above.

    • Bayesian Statistics Specialization by Herbert Lee from the Coursera learning platform. An excellent 5-part specialisation course which introduces and applies (using R) Bayesian data analytics. The course starts with basic concepts, such as selection priors, and moves on to more advanced topics, such as hierarchical models.

    Programming¶

    Books¶

    • Think Python: How to Think Like a Computer Scientist, 2nd Edition by Allen Downey. This book is a great introduction to computing science using Python. It assumes zero knowledge of Python.

    • Machine Learning from Scratch: Derivations in Concept and Code by Danny Friedman. This is an excellent book that teaches Python by applying machine learning models. The book first introduces the mathematics behind the machine learning models, followed by the Python implementation of the model. The entire book is a Jupyter notebook, and one can download the material.

    • Hands-On Programming with R by Garrett Grolemund. This book is a nice introduction to R programming for non-programmers. It assumes zero knowledge of R.

    • Hands-On Machine Learning with R by Bradley Boehmke & Brandon Greenwell. This is an excellent book which teaches R by applying machine learning models. The book first introduces the mathematics behind the machine learning models, followed by the R implementation of the model.

    Online Courses¶

    • Applied Software Engineering Fundamentals Specialization by Rav Ahuja. This 5-part specialisation course covers a variety of topics within software engineering, including Shell scripting, Git, and Python. It assumes zero knowledge of programming.

    • Crash Course on Python by Google. This course is great for those who are interested in learning only Python. Like the above course, it assumes zero prior knowledge of programming.

    • Python for Data Science and Machine Learning by Jose Portilla. This course starts from the absolute basics in Python (assumes no prior programming experience).

    • Deep Neural Networks with PyTorch by Joseph Santarcangelo This course introduces PyTorch (a Python library) by demonstrating how to implement deep learning models.

    • Introduction to TensorFlow for Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning by Laurence Moroney. This course introduces TensorFlow (a Python library) by demonstrating how to implement deep learning models.

    • Data Analysis with R Programming by Google. This course is great for those who are interested in learning only R. The course assumes zero prior knowledge.

    • Introduction to Big Data with Spark and Hadoop by Karthik Muthuraman. This is a great course for those interested in learning big data storage.

    • For those who are new to programming tools and want a concise, hands-on introduction, LinkedIn Learning can be very valuable. Imperial College London provides free access to this. LinkedIn Learning also offers courses on career development.

    • Deep Learning Basics by Master Year 2 Data Science from Institut Polytechnique de Paris

    • CS50’s Introduction to Artificial Intelligence with Python by Harvard University. 7-week introductory course.

    • 6.0001 Introduction to Computer Science and Programming in Python from MIT

    Other Topics¶

    Math Foundation for AI/ML¶

    • Mathematics for Machine Learning: Covers mathematical foundations and example machine learning algorithms that use these mathematical foundations. Free PDF.

    • Mathematical Foundations of Machine Learning: Online course covering linear algebra, calculus, and hands-on lessons in NumPy, TensorFlow, and PyTorch.

    Linear Algebra¶

    • Linear Algebra by Gilbert Strang. A comprehensive introduction for beginners.

    • Linear Algebra by MathTheBeautiful. YouTube series on linear algebra for absolute beginners.

    • Essence of linear algebra by 3Blue1Brown. YouTube series that goes over basic concepts with animations (geared toward beginners). Recommended for conceptual and visual understanding. Also check out a series on Differential equations, the Essence of calculus, and Neural networks (includes an intro to transformers).

    Optimisation¶

    • Convex Optimization I and Convex Optimization II by S. Boyd: Great content all over! Lectures 13-19 of COI and all COII are great and generally underrated. An online book is also available.

    • Numerical Optimal Control by M. Diehl and S. Gros: A great source on how to solve OC problems numerically, goes very well with his online course.

    • Nonlinear and Dynamic Optimization: From Theory to Practice by B. Chachuat: This is a great source for a formal and complete description of dynamic optimization problems in engineering practice. It may be more mathematically demanding than the three previous sources.

    Information theory¶

    • Information Theory, Pattern Recognition, and Neural Networks by David MacKay. A classical lecture series that uses information theory to introduce various models/algorithms. The entire lecture series is based on the book.

    Graph Theory¶

    • Introduction to Graph Theory by Robin J. Wilson. A comprehensive and easily accessible book, introducing all the relevant terms for the practitioner.

    Computer Vision¶

    • Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications by Richard Szeliski. Comprehensive textbook.

    Signal Processing¶

    • Audio Signal Processing for Machine Learning by Valerio Velardo. YouTube series on audio signal processing starting from the basics. There is also a Slack channel that you can join with a large community.

    Data Visualization¶

    • Data-Driven Animation for Science Communication: from conceptualization to asset creation, data visualization, and composition.
